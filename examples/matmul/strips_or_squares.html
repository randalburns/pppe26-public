Lecture 17, Step 6 · dividing C among threads

Strips or squares: how to cut up the output

Each thread computes its own piece of C. Cutting C in one direction gives strips. Cutting it in two directions gives squares. Both are correct. They differ in how much of A and B each thread has to read.

What a thread reads to compute its piece

To compute C[i][j] you need all of row i of A and all of column j of B. So a thread that owns a piece of C with r rows and c columns needs r whole rows of A and c whole columns of B.

work  = r × c × n     (the area of the piece, times n) reads = (r + c) × n   (the two edges of the piece, times n)

The work is fixed: with p threads, every thread gets 1/p of C. The reads are not fixed. They depend on the shape of the piece: on r + c, half of its perimeter.

Three ways to cut C for 4 threads

The ratios, for any number of threads

With p threads, a strip is n/p by n, and a square is n/√p by n/√p. Put those into reads = (r + c) × n:

strip:   (n/p + n) × n     = (1 + 1/p) matrices per thread square: (n/√p + n/√p) × n = 2/√p matrices per thread

A strip always reads more than one whole matrix, however many threads there are, because one of its edges is the full width of C. A square's edges both shrink as threads are added.

Smaller isn't better: many little tiles

The blog cuts C into 256 × 256 tiles and hands them to the threads in turn. For n = 1024 that is 16 tiles for 4 threads. Each tile is a small square, so each one reads little. But there are many of them, and every tile reads its own rows of A and columns of B again.

The tiles are slowest, as the count says: they read the most.

Rows beat squares, which the count does not predict. Two things explain it. First, at 4 threads the count barely separates them: 1.25 against 1.0. Second, the count treats every thread's reads as separate, and on this machine they aren't. The four fast cores share one cache. With rows, all four threads read the same part of B at the same time, so one copy in the shared cache serves all of them. That second reason is a likely explanation, not something we measured.

The ratio tells you how much each thread must read. Whether that costs anything depends on the machine: how many threads there are, and whether they share a cache. With many threads on separate caches, squares win. With 4 threads on one shared cache, strips are fine.
