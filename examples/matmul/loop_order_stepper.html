Lecture 17, Steps 2 and 5 · which loop goes inside

Same multiply-adds, different trips to memory

Step 2 put the j loop inside and got fast. Step 5 puts the k loop back inside and gets three times faster. Step both kernels together and watch where the numbers of C live while they are being summed.

Both panels do the same work at each step: 4 vector multiply-adds, each on 4 numbers. The 4 are independent, so the CPU can run them at the same time. After 16 steps each kernel has finished 16 numbers of C.

The picture is drawn small: n = 16, and the tiled kernel keeps a 2 × 8 block in 4 registers. The real kernel keeps a 4 × 16 block in 16 registers.

What to watch for

    
    
      
  • The row of C on the left never rests. Every step loads it from memory, adds one term, and stores it back. The same 16 numbers make that round trip 16 times.
    • 
      
  • The block of C on the right stays empty in memory until step 16. The sums are in the registers. C is loaded once before the k loop and stored once after it.
    • 
      
  • Both kernels run 4 multiply-adds at once. On the left they are 4 pieces of one row. On the right they are the 4 registers of one block. Neither kernel has one sum waiting on another.
    • 
      
  • The right side still walks down B. Each step jumps one row ahead. That is the cost that shows up at n = 2048, and it is what tiling k and packing fix.
    •

What goes wrong

        
    
          
  • The block changes every step. The j loop is inside, so after one term the kernel moves to the next block along the row. The registers are needed for that block.
    • 
          
  • So the block is stored after one term. Its sums are not finished. They go back to memory with 1 term of 16 added.
    • 
          
  • And loaded again on the next k. When k moves on, the kernel comes back to the same block, loads it, adds one more term, and stores it again.
    • 
          
  • The registers hold a block for one step, not sixteen. Watch the two blocks of C take turns. Above, with k inside, one block stayed put until it was done.
    • 
        

        

          
It does gain one thing over plain i, k, j. Each vector of B is used for 2 rows of C, so B is loaded half as often. The loads and stores of C are all still there.

A register can only save a trip to memory if the same numbers stay in it from one step to the next. With j inside, the numbers that stay the same from step to step are a whole row of C. At n = 1024 a row is 256 vectors, and the M5 has 32 vector registers.

          
With k inside, the numbers that stay are the block you chose. Choose 4 × 16 and it is 16 vectors, which fits. Holding C in registers and putting k inside are the same decision.
